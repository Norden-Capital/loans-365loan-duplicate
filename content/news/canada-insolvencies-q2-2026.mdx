**Canada insolvencies Q2 2026** data released by the Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) shows **37,523 consumer insolvency filings** between April and June — the most in any single quarter since 2009. The figures, published on 10 August and widely reported on 12 August, confirm that household financial stress has not eased through the first half of the year. For borrowers, the number is context rather than a verdict: it marks how many Canadians reached a formal debt solution, and those solutions exist precisely because the situation is recoverable.

## Canada Insolvencies Q2 2026: Summary of the New Data

- **37,523 consumer insolvencies** in Q2 2026, up **6.9% year over year** and **1.1% from Q1**
- Split into **8,600 bankruptcies** and **28,923 consumer proposals**
- **13,254 insolvencies filed in June alone**, up 5.7% from May and **11.5% year over year**
- **39,104 total insolvencies** including business filings of 1,281
- Ontario led all provinces with **15,029 filings**, up **10.0%** year over year

## Canada Insolvencies Q2 2026: The Headline Numbers

The OSB's quarterly release is the authoritative count of formal insolvency filings in Canada, covering both bankruptcies and consumer proposals filed under the *Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act*. The Canada insolvencies Q2 2026 tables break down as follows.

| Measure | Q2 2026 | Change vs Q1 2026 | Change vs Q2 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer insolvencies | **37,523** | +1.1% | **+6.9%** |
| — Consumer bankruptcies | 8,600 | +13.5% | — |
| — Consumer proposals | 28,923 | −2.1% | — |
| Business insolvencies | 1,281 | +4.0% | +0.2% |
| **Total insolvencies** | **39,104** | — | — |

Over the twelve months ending 30 June 2026, the OSB recorded **145,762 consumer filings** and 4,743 business filings.

## Why "Since 2009" Is the Right Comparison

The 2009 benchmark matters because it is the last time Canadian households filed at this rate, during the aftermath of the global financial crisis. Reaching a comparable level without a comparable financial shock is what makes the current data notable.

Charles St-Arnaud, chief economist at Servus Credit Union, told BNN Bloomberg that "high household debt and stagnating purchasing power have put pressure on Canadians' finances in recent years." He also observed that insolvency rates "appear to be stabilizing near levels last seen in 2009" — a useful counterweight to any reading of the Canada insolvencies Q2 2026 figures as an accelerating collapse.

Both readings can be true. The level is historically high; the rate of increase has moderated from the sharper climbs recorded earlier in the cycle.

## The Detail Most Coverage Skipped

The quarter-over-quarter headline is a modest 1.1% rise. Underneath it, the composition shifted more than the total did.

- **Consumer bankruptcies rose from 7,576 in Q1 to 8,600 in Q2** — an increase of roughly 13.5%
- **Consumer proposals fell from 29,545 to 28,923** — a decline of about 2.1%

That matters because the two options are not interchangeable. A consumer proposal requires a debtor to commit to affordable monthly payments over a period of up to five years, which presupposes enough stable income to sustain them. Bankruptcy does not carry the same requirement.

A shift in the mix toward bankruptcy, while the overall total barely moves, is consistent with a group of filers who no longer have the room in their budget that a proposal assumes. It is a quieter signal than the headline, and arguably a more informative one.

## Background: How the Numbers Got Here

Canadian insolvency filings have climbed steadily through the current cycle. The network's earlier reporting on [Q1 2026 insolvencies](/news/canada-consumer-insolvencies-2026) recorded 37,121 consumer filings — itself described at the time as the highest since 2009. Q2 has now edged past it.

The pressures behind the trend are familiar and cumulative rather than sudden: elevated housing costs, several years of above-target inflation, and the lagged effect of interest rate increases working through renewals and revolving credit. The Bank of Canada's policy rate has been held at 2.25% since July, but a rate hold does not reverse debt already carried at higher costs.

## Why This Matters for Households

For most readers the Canada insolvencies Q2 2026 release is not personal news at all. It is, however, a useful marker of where the pressure is landing — and of the fact that formal remedies are being used at scale.

Three practical implications:

1. **Insolvency is a legal process with a defined end, not a permanent state.** A consumer proposal typically remains on a credit file for about three years after completion; a first bankruptcy for six to seven years after discharge.
2. **The free advice arrives earliest.** A Licensed Insolvency Trustee provides a free initial consultation and is the only professional who can file either process. Non-profit credit counselling agencies help at little or no cost.
3. **Waiting narrows the options.** A proposal depends on being able to sustain payments. Households that act while they still have budget room have more routes available than those who arrive with none.

## Canada Insolvencies Q2 2026: Key Takeaways

- Consumer filings hit **37,523** in Q2 2026, the highest quarterly figure since 2009
- The year-over-year increase was **6.9%**; the quarter-over-quarter increase just **1.1%**
- **Bankruptcies rose sharply while proposals dipped**, suggesting deeper strain among new filers
- June's monthly figure of **13,254** was up 11.5% from a year earlier
- One economist describes the rate as **stabilizing** at 2009-era levels rather than accelerating

## Conclusion

The Canada insolvencies Q2 2026 data confirms a plateau at an uncomfortable altitude. Filings are the highest they have been since the financial crisis, but the pace of deterioration has slowed, and the shift from proposals toward bankruptcies is the detail worth watching in the third quarter. For anyone carrying debt that is becoming unmanageable, the most useful takeaway is that the earliest conversations are the free ones — and that they open more doors than late ones do.
