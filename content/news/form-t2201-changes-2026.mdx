Two administrative deadlines are quietly changing how **Form T2201** must be submitted, and neither has had much coverage. From **8 September 2026** the Canada Revenue Agency will stop accepting any version printed before 2023. Since **14 July 2026** the "submit documents" option in CRA online accounts no longer takes disability tax credit paperwork at all. Both matter more than a form change normally would, because this form is the gate to a monthly benefit.

![Form T2201 application paperwork and a pen on a desk before submission to the CRA](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8441775/pexels-photo-8441775.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Form T2201: Summary of the Changes

- **From 8 September 2026:** pre-2023 versions **will no longer be accepted**
- **Since 14 July 2026:** the CRA account **"submit documents"** option no longer accepts DTC documents
- Older versions are rejected because they **do not meet updated requirements**
- The **digital application** is always the current version and is processed faster
- An approved certificate is the **prerequisite** for the Canada Disability Benefit

## What Is Actually Changing

The CRA's position on the first change is straightforward: applications submitted on a version of Form T2201 printed before 2023 will no longer be accepted beginning **8 September 2026**, because those versions do not meet current requirements. If an older one arrives after that date, the agency may return the application and ask for the latest version.

The practical risk is not carelessness — it is a stale download. A copy saved to a computer eighteen months ago, or a photocopy held at a clinic, looks perfectly complete. The version date is printed on the form, and it is the only thing separating an accepted submission from a returned one.

The second change is less publicised and is already in force. Since **14 July 2026**, the "submit documents" function in CRA online accounts no longer accepts DTC-related documents, including the application itself. Anyone who used that route previously will find it simply no longer applies — the kind of change that produces a silent failure rather than an error message.

## What Most Form T2201 Guides Still Get Wrong

This is worth stating plainly, because it affects anyone researching the topic today. A number of widely-read pages about Form T2201 still tell readers the following:

> "It cannot be submitted online. It must be printed, filled out, and mailed or delivered to the Canada Revenue Agency."

**That is out of date.** The CRA operates a fully digital application. You complete your portion online through your CRA account, your medical practitioner completes **Part B** online via the CRA's DTC digital application page, and once they finish it the form is **submitted to the CRA automatically**. The agency states that online submissions are processed more quickly than paper.

Two consequences follow:

1. **Do not assume you must mail it.** The digital route is faster and removes the version problem entirely, because the online form is always the current version.
2. **Treat any guide that says "mail only" as stale on everything else too.** A page that has not been updated for the digital application has almost certainly not been updated for the 8 September deadline either.

The form's two-part structure has not changed: **Part A** is completed by the person with the disability or their legal representative, **Part B** by the medical practitioner. What has changed is how those parts reach the CRA.

## Why a Form Deadline Is a Benefits Story

This is the part that makes a Form T2201 submission consequential rather than clerical, and it is why the September deadline is worth acting on now.

An approved disability tax credit certificate is the **mandatory prerequisite** for the **Canada Disability Benefit**. For the July 2026 to June 2027 period that benefit pays up to **$204.20 per month** to eligible people aged 18 to 64, and a one-time supplemental payment of **$150** is being paid from September 2026.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Canada Disability Benefit maximum | **$204.20/month** (Jul 2026 – Jun 2027) |
| One-time supplement | **$150**, from September 2026 |
| Age range | 18 to 64 |
| Mandatory prerequisite | **Approved DTC certificate** |
| Full amount below | Adjusted family net income of $23,000 |
| Reduces to zero at | $35,000, or $45,000 where at least $10,000 is working income |

So a returned form is not a delayed tax credit. It is delayed benefit money — and because the benefit runs on a July-to-June cycle and depends on filed tax returns, a hold-up at the application stage can push entitlement back considerably further than the processing time alone suggests.

The certificate also unlocks other measures, including the Registered Disability Savings Plan and the disability supplement of the Canada Workers Benefit. One approval, several consequences.

## Background: A Slow Move to Digital

The Form T2201 deadlines are the tail end of a multi-year shift rather than a sudden policy change. The CRA introduced a fully digital application for medical practitioners, and the September cut-off is the point at which it stops maintaining compatibility with the pre-2023 paperwork that system was built to replace.

The form itself was also revised. Improvements made in 2024 to both the paper and digital versions included clearer questions on Type 1 diabetes and space to provide examples under the cumulative-effects category — changes intended to reduce the back-and-forth that has historically slowed DTC decisions.

## Why the Form T2201 Deadlines Matter

Four practical implications for anyone applying, or helping someone apply.

1. **Download it fresh, today.** Do not reuse a saved copy. The version date is printed on the form and must be 2023 or later.
2. **Prefer the digital route.** It is always current, it removes the version question, and the CRA processes it faster.
3. **Tell the medical practitioner which route you are using.** Part B is theirs to complete, and a clinic holding old paper stock is a common point of failure.
4. **File your tax return on time.** The Canada Disability Benefit depends on filed returns — yours and your spouse's if you have one. An approved certificate alone does not produce payment.

## Form T2201: Key Takeaways

- **8 September 2026** is the cut-off for pre-2023 versions
- The CRA account **"submit documents"** option stopped accepting DTC paperwork on **14 July 2026**
- It **can** be submitted online, despite what many guides still say
- An approved certificate gates the **Canada Disability Benefit** — up to **$204.20/month** plus a **$150** supplement from September
- A returned form delays benefit money, not just a tax credit

## Conclusion

Form deadlines rarely make news, and this one deserves to. The changes do not alter who qualifies or how much anyone receives — they determine whether a submitted application is looked at, and the cost of getting it wrong falls on people whose income is already fixed.

The fix takes five minutes: download the current version, or use the digital application and skip the version question altogether. Anyone with an application in progress on older paperwork should replace it before 8 September.
