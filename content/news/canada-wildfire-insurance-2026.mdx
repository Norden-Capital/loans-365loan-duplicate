**Canada wildfire insurance 2026** is shaping up to be one of the fastest-rising fixed costs in the household budget — and a hot, dry summer is the reason. Forecasters point to intense summer heat and lower-than-normal rainfall pushing fire danger up across much of the country, and Canada's 2026 wildfire season is on track to be another severe one. That matters for your wallet, not just the map: after years of record catastrophe losses, insurers are passing the bill to homeowners, and premiums are climbing again this year.

![Canada wildfire insurance 2026 — a homeowner reviewing a rising home insurance renewal and household budget](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8292883/pexels-photo-8292883.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## What's Driving Canada Wildfire Insurance 2026 Higher

The short version: climate-driven disasters are getting more expensive, and insurance prices follow the losses. According to the **Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC)**, insured catastrophe losses have **topped $2 billion** in each of the last several years and climbed toward **nearly $10 billion** a couple of years ago — a run that pushes premiums up nationwide. When insurers pay out more for floods, storms and wildfires, they recover it at renewal, from policyholders.

None of that is easing this year. Canada's wildfire tracker points to a season fuelled by intense heat and a rainfall deficit — conditions that raise fire danger and, with it, the risk insurers are pricing in. For homeowners, canada wildfire insurance 2026 is less a headline about faraway fires and more a line item that keeps getting bigger.

## A Fixed Cost That's Outrunning Inflation

Here's the part that hits the budget. Home and mortgage insurance premiums rose **45% between December 2019 and December 2025** — more than **twice** the increase in the all-items Consumer Price Index over the same stretch, according to Statistics Canada figures reported by CBC News. In plain terms, the cost of protecting your home has grown at roughly double the pace of everything else.

That's what makes this a cost-of-living story. Unlike a night out or a subscription you can cancel, insurance is a **fixed cost** — you pay it to keep a roof over your head and, if you carry a mortgage, to keep your lender onside. When a fixed cost outruns inflation, it quietly crowds out everything else in the budget.

## The 2025 Season Left a Mark — Especially for Cabin Owners

![Charred trees and smoke after a wildfire, illustrating the property losses pushing insurance premiums higher](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6863515/pexels-photo-6863515.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

The 2025 wildfire season is a preview of the pressure heading into this one. In **Manitoba alone**, fires destroyed **at least 130 properties**, displaced about **33,000 people** and burned roughly **4% of the province**, according to CBC News. Losses on that scale don't stay local — they feed into the risk models insurers use to set prices everywhere.

Cabin and cottage owners are especially exposed. Recreational properties often sit in or near forested, higher-risk zones, and many homeowners with a second property should expect **higher premiums in 2026** as insurers reprice wildfire risk. For families already stretched, a jump on two properties at once can sting.
