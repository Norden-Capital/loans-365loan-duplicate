goeasy Q2 2026 results, released on August 6, contain a number that stands out even in a quarter full of them: **gross loan originations fell 70%, to $272.1 million from $903.7 million** a year earlier. Canada's largest non-prime consumer lender did not merely have a difficult quarter — it deliberately stopped writing new loans at close to the rate it had been.

## The goeasy Q2 2026 headline numbers

| Measure | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $390.0M | $431.3M | −10% |
| Net income | **$15.9M** | $91.5M | **−83%** |
| Diluted EPS | $0.96 | $5.49 | −82% |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $1.02 | $4.40 | −77% |
| **Gross loan originations** | **$272.1M** | **$903.7M** | **−70%** |
| Gross consumer loans receivable | $5.00B | $5.11B | −2% |
| Net charge-off rate | **16.7%** | 8.7% | **+800 bps** |
| Allowance for credit losses | $499.5M | $406.7M | +23% |

The pairing in rows five and six is the story. **Originations fell 70% while the loan book fell only 2%** — meaning the existing portfolio is still being collected while very little new lending is replacing it.

## goeasy Q2 2026: the pullback was a decision, not a drought

It would be easy to read a 70% origination decline as collapsing demand. goeasy's own disclosure describes something different: a deliberate contraction.

The company points to a **six-point action plan**, and the elements it highlighted for the quarter were explicit:

- **Managing origination activity to prioritise liquidity** — lending less on purpose to preserve cash.
- **Tighter credit underwriting** on merchant-originated automotive and powersports loans.
- **Moderating direct-to-consumer originations.**

Alongside that, goeasy **repaid the full $314.0 million balance** on its revolving credit facility and **regained access to incremental draws as of July 1**. Its debt-to-adjusted-tangible-equity ratio improved to **4.95x from 5.30x** in Q1 2026.

> Those are the actions of a lender rebuilding its balance sheet rather than one that ran out of customers.

## Why credit losses forced the issue

The tightening reported in goeasy Q2 2026 did not happen in isolation. **The net charge-off rate reached 16.7%, up from 8.7%** — roughly double in twelve months. That means a substantially larger share of the loan book was written off as uncollectable.

The allowance for credit losses rose to **$499.5 million**, or **9.99% of gross receivables**. In plain terms, goeasy is now setting aside close to a tenth of its entire loan book against expected losses.

A lender facing losses at that level has limited options: raise pricing, tighten who it approves, or shrink. The Q2 numbers suggest goeasy chose the second and third together.

## What goeasy Q2 2026 means for borrowers

This is where the earnings release becomes consumer news rather than investor news.

**Approval standards at one of Canada's largest non-prime lenders have tightened materially.** Borrowers who would have been approved a year ago may not be now — particularly for **automotive and powersports loans through merchants**, which the company named specifically.

Three practical implications:

1. **A decline that would have been an approval last year is now plausible** at this lender, without anything having changed in your own file.
2. **Do not read one lender as the whole market.** Competitor Propel Holdings reported the opposite in the same quarter — originations **up 25%** and record revenue. Supply has shifted between lenders rather than simply vanishing.
3. **Shop more widely than usual.** When one large lender retrenches, the spread between lenders' criteria widens, and a single decline carries less information about your overall prospects.

## The dividend signal

The Board **indefinitely suspended the regular quarterly dividend** and share repurchases under its normal course issuer bid.

For a company that had paid and grown a dividend for years, suspension is among the strongest signals a board can send about capital priorities. Read alongside the revolver repayment and the improved leverage ratio, it points consistently in one direction: **capital preservation ahead of growth**.

## Guidance: the contraction is expected to continue

goeasy's full-year 2026 outlook:

- **Gross consumer loans receivable: $4.8–$5.0 billion.** Against $5.00 billion at quarter-end, that range implies flat to shrinking.
- **Total yield on consumer loans: 26.5–28.0%.**
- **Net charge-off rate: 14.5–16.0%.**

That last figure matters most. The company is forecasting charge-offs to stay well above the 8.7% of a year ago for the remainder of 2026 — elevated losses treated as the near-term normal, not a one-quarter shock.

## How goeasy Q2 2026 compares with its closest rival

The clearest way to read this quarter is beside the competitor that reported one day earlier.

| Measure | goeasy | Propel Holdings |
|---|---|---|
| Originations | **−70%** | **+25%** |
| Net charge-offs | 16.7% (from 8.7%) | 12% (stable) |
| Net income | $15.9M (−83%) | $16.2M (+7%) |
| Dividend | **Suspended** | **Raised — 12th straight** |

Two things stand out. The companies earned **almost identical net income** despite goeasy carrying a loan book roughly eight times larger. And their credit losses moved in opposite directions from a broadly similar borrower base — which suggests underwriting and product mix, not the economy alone, explain the gap.

## goeasy Q2 2026: key takeaways

- **goeasy Q2 2026 originations fell 70%** to $272.1 million; the loan book fell only 2%.
- **Net income dropped 83%** to $15.9 million; revenue fell 10% to $390.0 million.
- **Net charge-offs hit 16.7%**, roughly double a year earlier; allowance rate 9.99%.
- **The dividend and buyback were suspended indefinitely.**
- **Leverage improved to 4.95x**; the $314.0 million revolver was fully repaid.
- **Full-year guidance implies a flat-to-smaller loan book** and charge-offs of 14.5–16.0%.

## The bottom line

goeasy Q2 2026 is a lender choosing balance-sheet safety over growth, and doing it decisively — a 70% cut in new lending, a suspended dividend, a repaid revolver, and guidance that expects elevated losses to persist.

For borrowers, the practical consequence is narrower than the headlines suggest but real: credit is tighter at one of the country's biggest non-prime lenders, and noticeably so in specific product lines. It is not evidence that borrowing has become impossible — a direct competitor grew originations 25% in the same three months. It is evidence that where you apply now matters more than it did a year ago.
