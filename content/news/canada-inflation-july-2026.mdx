**Canada inflation July 2026** came in at 3.0% year over year, up from 2.8% in June and landing exactly at the top of the Bank of Canada's control range. Statistics Canada published the figure on **August 17, 2026**. The detail beneath the headline is more reassuring than the number itself: almost the entire increase came from two volatile categories.

![Canada inflation July 2026 — a household reviewing rising prices and monthly budget pressure at a kitchen table](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4098152/pexels-photo-4098152.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Summary

- Headline CPI **+3.0% y/y** in July, from **+2.8%** in June
- Month over month **+0.5%** (**+0.3%** seasonally adjusted)
- **Gasoline +25.7%** and **travel tours +15.2%** drove the acceleration
- CPI **excluding gasoline +2.2%** — the third consecutive month at that rate
- Bank of Canada core measures averaged **2.0%**
- Next rate decision: **September 2, 2026**

## What the canada inflation july 2026 report showed

Statistics Canada said higher prices for gasoline and travel tours in July contributed to the acceleration in the headline index. The agency's figures show gasoline up **25.7%** from a year earlier, compared with **20.5%** in June — a swing driven largely by comparison against weaker prices in mid-2025.

Travel tours rose **15.2%** year over year, up sharply from **6.8%** in June. Air transportation climbed **12.0%**, against **9.6%** the month before. Reporting on the release linked the travel increase to higher hotel and flight costs for Canadians travelling to the United States, including cities hosting the 2026 FIFA World Cup.

Transportation as a whole was up **7.8%**. Those three lines account for most of the gap between the canada inflation july 2026 headline and the underlying trend.

## Canada inflation July 2026: the categories that eased

While the headline rose, several everyday categories moved the other way.

| Component | July 2026 (y/y) | June 2026 (y/y) | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-items CPI | **3.0%** | 2.8% | ▲ |
| CPI excluding gasoline | **2.2%** | 2.2% | ◆ flat |
| Gasoline | **25.7%** | 20.5% | ▲ |

Grocery inflation slowed to **3.1%**, down from **3.9%**. Shelter — the largest single line in most household budgets — rose just **1.3%**.

That combination matters for how you read canada inflation july 2026 against your own budget. A household that does not drive much and did not take a summer holiday abroad likely experienced something closer to the 2.2% excluding-gasoline figure than the 3.0% headline.

## Background: what the target actually is

The Bank of Canada aims for **2% inflation**, measured by the total CPI, within a **control range of 1% to 3%**. A 3.0% reading is therefore at the top edge of that range, not above it — a distinction frequently lost in coverage.

The Bank also watches preferred core measures that strip out the most volatile price movements. In July, **CPI-median was 2.0%** and **CPI-trim was 1.9%**, averaging **2.0%** against 1.9% in June. Those measures sit at the midpoint of the control range.

RBC Economics summarised the release as inflation edging higher "while underlying pressures remain contained."

## Why this matters

For borrowers, the practical question is what the reading does to interest rates. Two features of this report point the same way.

First, the acceleration was concentrated in **gasoline and travel** — categories the Bank of Canada explicitly discounts because they swing on factors monetary policy cannot influence. Second, both drivers have a plausible expiry. Gasoline's year-over-year jump reflects comparison against a weak base in 2025, and World Cup-related travel demand is a one-off event, not a recurring monthly cost.

Meanwhile the measures the Bank actually steers by held near 2%.

The next scheduled decision is **September 2, 2026**. Several economists have suggested the policy rate is likely to remain unchanged given core inflation near target, though the Bank has issued no signal and the decision will also weigh employment and growth data.

For households, the more useful takeaway is that Canada inflation July 2026 was not a broad-based price surge. Groceries decelerated and shelter costs rose slowly. Fuel is the line item that moved.

## Canada inflation july 2026: key takeaways

1. **3.0% is the ceiling of the target range, not a breach of it.** The Bank of Canada's range is 1% to 3%.
2. **Strip out gasoline and inflation has been flat at 2.2% for three months.** The underlying trend has not changed.
3. **Core measures averaged 2.0%** — CPI-median 2.0%, CPI-trim 1.9%.
4. **Groceries eased to 3.1%** from 3.9%, and shelter rose only 1.3%.
5. **Watch September 2** for the Bank of Canada's next rate decision.

## Conclusion

The July report is a case study in reading past a headline. The number that will be quoted is 3.0%, and it is accurate. But the composition shows an economy where underlying price pressure held near target while fuel and holiday travel did the work.

For anyone budgeting month to month, the practical implication is narrow rather than broad: if your costs rose noticeably in July, the odds are it showed up at the pump rather than across the grocery bill.
