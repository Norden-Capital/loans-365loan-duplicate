**Canada average weekly earnings 2026** data landed on July 30, and the picture is one of slow, unspectacular improvement. Statistics Canada reported average weekly earnings of **$1,337.77** in May 2026, up **3.4%** from a year earlier, while payroll employment added just 24,100 positions and job vacancies stayed flat for a fifth straight month. Pay is rising faster than the Bank of Canada's 2% inflation target — but not fast enough, and not evenly enough, to resolve the affordability squeeze most households describe.

*Reported July 31, 2026 from Statistics Canada's release of July 30, 2026. Figures are for the May 2026 reference month and may be revised. This is news reporting and general information, not financial advice.*

## The 5 key numbers behind Canada average weekly earnings 2026

Statistics Canada's Survey of Employment, Payrolls and Hours (SEPH) covers non-farm payroll employees and is the country's most detailed read on what employers actually pay. Here is what the May 2026 release shows.

| Measure | May 2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---|
| Average weekly earnings | $1,337.77 | +3.4% year over year |
| Payroll employment | +24,100 | +0.1% month over month |
| Payroll employment (annual) | +112,500 | +0.6% year over year |
| Job vacancies | 495,700 | Flat, 5th consecutive month |
| Largest monthly sector gain | Public administration | +11,700 positions |

The one negative worth flagging: **finance and insurance shed 5,700 positions** in the month, one of the sharper declines among major sectors. Health care and social assistance continued its upward employment trend, though vacancies in that sector fell notably.

## Why the Canada average weekly earnings 2026 figure is only half the story

A 3.4% annual gain looks healthy against a 2% inflation target. Two things complicate how the Canada average weekly earnings 2026 headline should be read.

**First, averages are not paycheques.** The SEPH average blends every payroll sector, from public administration to retail. A national number rising 3.4% tells you nothing about whether your own employer moved.

**Second, workers have limited leverage.** Job vacancies have now been essentially flat for five consecutive months at 495,700, and unchanged from a year ago. Vacancies are the clearest signal of employer demand. When they stop rising, the ability to negotiate a raise or move jobs for more money weakens — which is why steady headline wage growth can coexist with households feeling no better off.

That gap shows up directly in survey data. TransUnion's Q2 2026 Consumer Pulse study, released July 22, found **50% of Canadians say their income is not keeping pace with inflation**, and 86% ranked inflation among their top three financial concerns — even as 45% expressed optimism about the year ahead.

## Background: what the Bank of Canada is watching

The Canada average weekly earnings 2026 series feeds directly into monetary policy. In its **Summary of Governing Council deliberations published July 29**, the Bank of Canada noted that excess supply in the economy and **moderate wage growth** supported its view that inflation will ease to about 2½% in the second half of 2026 before returning to the 2% target in early 2027.

Put plainly: the Bank regards pay growth at this pace as consistent with inflation continuing to cool. Wage gains large enough to genuinely outrun living costs would complicate that outlook. The Bank held its policy rate at **2¼%** on July 15.

## Why this matters for household budgets

For anyone managing a tight month, the practical takeaway from the Canada average weekly earnings 2026 data is that **income growth is real but slow, and job-market mobility is limited**. Neither a raise nor a job change is a reliable plan for closing a gap in 2026.

That matters for short-term borrowing in a specific, mechanical way. Licensed payday lenders in Canada size a loan against your net pay for a single pay period — capped at 50% of it in Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta and Saskatchewan, and 30% in Manitoba, with a $1,500 statutory ceiling. Earnings that rise 3.4% a year move that limit very little. Our guide to [payday loan regulations in Canada](/blog/payday-loan-regulations-canada) sets out how those caps interact.

The federal cost cap is the other half of the equation. Since January 1, 2025, the total cost of borrowing on a payday loan cannot exceed **$14 per $100** advanced — roughly a 365% APR on a 14-day loan. Slow wage growth does not change that price.

## Key takeaways

- **Canada average weekly earnings 2026 reached $1,337.77 in May**, up 3.4% year over year.
- **Payroll employment grew 0.1%** in the month and 0.6% over the year — positive, but marginal.
- **Job vacancies are flat at 495,700**, a fifth consecutive month, limiting worker bargaining power.
- **Finance and insurance lost 5,700 positions**; public administration added 11,700.
- Half of Canadians still report income failing to keep pace with inflation, per TransUnion.

## What to watch next

Three things will shape whether the Canada average weekly earnings 2026 trend translates into genuine relief. Watch whether **job vacancies break out of their five-month plateau**, which would signal renewed employer demand. Watch the **Bank of Canada's next rate decision**, since the Council has said it will adjust policy as needed. And watch **inflation prints through the second half of 2026** against the Bank's 2½% expectation.

Until those move, the sensible reading of Canada average weekly earnings 2026 is that pay rises slowly and costs do not fall.
