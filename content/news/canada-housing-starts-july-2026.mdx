## Brief summary

**Canada housing starts July 2026** data, released by Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation on August 18, show the seasonally adjusted annual rate falling **5%** to **229,074 units** from 240,773 in June. Actual starts in centres of 10,000 or more came in at **18,834 units**, down **19%** against July 2025. Vancouver fell **42%** year over year while Montreal rose **3%**.

## Introduction

New home construction slowed again last month. The monthly figure is volatile and often overread, so the useful question is not whether it fell but *which* measures fell and by how much — because the answers point in noticeably different directions.

## The main story: what Canada housing starts July 2026 showed

CMHC released the July data on **August 18, 2026**. The headline numbers:

| Measure | July 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonally adjusted annual rate, all areas | **229,074 units** | **−5%** from 240,773 in June |
| Actual starts, centres 10,000+ | **18,834 units** | **−19%** vs 23,155 in July 2025 |
| Year-to-date actual starts | **131,851 units** | **−4%** vs 2025 |
| Six-month moving average (trend) | **247,377 units** | **−0.5%** from June |
| Rural starts estimate (annual rate) | 11,274 units | — |
| Completions, centres 50,000+ | 19,773 units | **+8.1%** from June |
| Units under construction, centres 50,000+ | 373,091 units | +0.6% month over month |
| Approved units not yet started | 141,480 units | +3% month over month |

**Tania Bourassa-Ochoa, Deputy Chief Economist at CMHC**, said in the release that "July's results show housing starts continuing to moderate as new home construction evolves per CMHC's recent Summer Update."

### The city spread is the real story in Canada housing starts July 2026

Among major centres, the year-over-year picture diverges sharply:

| Major centre | Year-over-year change in starts |
|---|---|
| **Vancouver** | **−42%** |
| **Toronto** | **−10%** |
| **Montreal** | **+3%** |

A national decline of 19% in actual starts is an average of three very different markets. Vancouver's drop is more than four times Toronto's, and Montreal moved in the opposite direction entirely.

## Background: how Canada housing starts July 2026 is measured

CMHC publishes housing starts monthly and reports several measures at once because they answer different questions. The **seasonally adjusted annual rate** projects one month's activity across a full year, which makes it responsive but jumpy. The **six-month moving average** strips that volatility out.

That distinction matters here. The annual rate fell 5%, but the trend measure fell only **0.5%**. On the trend, this is gradual moderation — not the sharp reversal the headline implies.

The data also lands against CMHC's **Housing Market Outlook Summer Update**, published **July 22, 2026**, which forecast weak activity through the rest of the year with gradual improvement into 2027 and 2028. **Kevin Hughes, Deputy Chief Economist at CMHC**, said in that update: "Price reductions have not yet been enough to bring demand back into the market as economic uncertainty, income growth and borrowing conditions all have played a role in sidelining buyers."

Timeline of the sequence:

1. **July 22, 2026** — CMHC Summer Update forecasts continued weakness in starts and prices through 2026
2. **August 18, 2026** — July housing starts released, showing a 5% monthly decline
3. **Mid-September 2026** — August data expected

## Why Canada housing starts July 2026 matters to renters

For most households the connection is indirect but real, and it runs through **rent** rather than through house prices.

Starts today become finished units in roughly two to three years. A weaker month of **Canada housing starts July 2026** therefore has no effect on this autumn's rents or on anyone's current housing costs. What it does is shape the supply pipeline for **2028 and beyond** — and rental supply is the channel through which construction data eventually reaches renters.

Three points worth separating:

- **Near-term supply is holding up.** Completions rose 8.1% in July and 373,091 units remain under construction. Units are still being finished and delivered.
- **The pipeline is thinning at the front.** Fewer starts now means fewer completions later, and 141,480 approved units are sitting unstarted — permits exist, construction has not begun.
- **Where you live matters more than the national figure.** A Vancouver renter and a Montreal renter are looking at opposite trends.

For anyone budgeting around housing costs, the practical takeaway is that this release changes nothing this month. It is a signal about the medium term, and it is a regional signal rather than a national one.

## Key takeaways

- Seasonally adjusted annual rate: **229,074 units**, down **5%** from June
- Actual starts in centres 10,000+: **18,834**, down **19%** year over year
- Year-to-date starts: **131,851**, down **4%** on 2025
- Trend measure down only **0.5%** — moderation, not collapse
- **Vancouver −42%, Toronto −10%, Montreal +3%** year over year
- Completions **rose 8.1%**, so near-term delivery is not the problem
- **141,480 approved units have not been started**

## Conclusion

The Canada housing starts July 2026 report is best read as two separate signals. The monthly rate dropped 5% and actual starts were down 19% on last year, which is genuinely weak. But the trend measure barely moved, completions increased, and more than 370,000 units remain under construction across the country.

The number to watch next is not starts but **the gap between approved-but-unstarted units and actual starts**. At 141,480 units and rising, that backlog is where the effect of financing costs and construction economics is currently showing up most clearly — and it will tell us more about 2028 rents than any single month's headline.
