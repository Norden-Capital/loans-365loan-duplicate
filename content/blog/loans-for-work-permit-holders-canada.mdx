Loans for work permit holders Canada lenders decline are usually rejected on a **date**, not on the money. The applicant earns enough, has worked for the same employer for two years and has never missed a payment — and an automated system reads an expiry date that has passed, or that lands before the final instalment, and stops there. This guide is about the documents that answer the date question, including one that most borrowers do not know exists.

## Quick Answer

If you applied to renew before your permit expired and stayed in Canada, you are on **maintained status**: legally in the country and, under paragraph R186(u), still authorised to work the same job for the same employer. IRCC issues an **interim proof of work letter (WP-EXT)** as evidence — and since **27 April 2026** most of those letters carry a **365-day** validity instead of 180. Lead your application with that letter, disclose the maintained-status situation before the system finds it, and size the term to the timeline you can prove.

## Why Loans for Work Permit Holders Canada Fail the Date Check

Underwriting is a series of yes/no gates, and several of them are dates. A permit expiry that precedes the last scheduled payment triggers a repayment-continuity flag. An expired SIN triggers an identity flag. A renewal in progress with no supporting letter triggers both at once, because the file shows expired documents and nothing that supersedes them.

None of those flags is a judgement about you. They are the system doing what it was built to do — and the reason applications for loans for work permit holders Canada wide fail at a rate that has nothing to do with the applicant's finances.

> **The paradox at the centre of this:** on maintained status you can be fully legal, fully employed and fully paid, while every dated document in your wallet says "expired." Resolving that gap is the entire job.

## Maintained Status: Legal to Work, "Expired" on Paper

Almost everyone who researches loans for work permit holders Canada offers during a renewal runs into this term without a clear definition of it, so here it is precisely.

Maintained status (the term that replaced "implied status") applies when three things are true at once:

1. You **applied to extend before** your existing permit expired. A late filing — even by a day — breaks the protection entirely.
2. You **remained in Canada** after the expiry.
3. You **continue to comply** with the conditions of the expired permit.

Under R186(u) that means continuing **the same job, for the same employer, in the same location, under the same conditions** as the expired permit specified. It is not a licence to change roles while you wait. Two further limits matter for anyone planning around a loan: leaving and re-entering Canada puts maintained status at risk, and if the renewal is refused you must stop working immediately on being notified.

## The WP-EXT Letter: 365 Days of Proof Since April 2026

This is the document that closes the gap, and it is genuinely under-known outside HR and immigration circles.

When you apply to renew, IRCC issues an **interim proof of work letter**, commonly called a **WP-EXT letter**. It confirms you may continue working while the application is processed, and it is the evidence employers, Service Canada and provincial authorities are told to accept.

In a program delivery update dated **27 April 2026**, IRCC **extended the validity of most of these letters from 180 days to 365 days** from the date the application is received. The change clarified existing rules rather than altering the law — but for a borrower the practical effect is large, because it turns a six-month piece of paper into a twelve-month one. If IRCC has not finalised the renewal by the date shown, you can still keep working.

One exception worth knowing: **post-graduation work permit applicants remain on the 180-day cycle.** If you are transitioning from a study permit, do not assume the 365-day figure applies to you.

For a lender, this letter does the single thing the permit cannot: it puts a **future** date on the file. That is why, among all the paperwork behind loans for work permit holders Canada lenders assess, this one letter changes more outcomes than anything else you can supply.

## Your SIN Expires With Your Permit — and You Can't Renew It Yet

Here is the trap that catches almost everyone, because the rule is genuinely counter-intuitive.

Temporary residents are issued a SIN beginning with **9**, and it expires on the **same day as the immigration document** used to obtain it. When your permit lapses into maintained status, the SIN lapses with it — and **you cannot update the SIN expiry date while you are on maintained status**, because Service Canada needs a new immigration document to update it against.

So you are left holding an expired SIN you are not allowed to fix. IRCC's guidance addresses this directly: *"If you applied to extend your work permit before your initial work permit expired, you can keep working, even if your SIN is expired."*

Three consequences follow:

- **Do not treat the expired SIN as disqualifying.** It is expected in this window.
- **Disclose it before the system flags it.** An unexplained expired SIN on an application looks like an error; an explained one with a letter attached looks like paperwork.
- **Once your new permit arrives, update the SIN immediately** — and note that you must give the new expiry date to your employer **within 3 days**.

## The Timing Rule: Apply Before the Gap, Not During It

This is the most actionable thing in the article, and it follows directly from everything above.

Your documentary position is **strongest in the months before your permit expires** and **weakest during maintained status**. Before expiry, every document you hold shows a future date and any system reads them cleanly. During maintained status, you are just as legal — but your permit and your SIN both show past dates, and you are relying on a letter to explain the gap.

So if you can foresee a borrowing need — a car, a move, consolidating a balance — the cheapest version of that decision is the one made **while your permit is still current**.

| Timing | Documentary strength | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| 6+ months before expiry | Strongest | Standard process; no expiry conversation at all |
| 1–3 months before expiry | Good | Short term approves easily; longer terms get questions |
| Renewal filed, permit not yet expired | Good | You hold both a valid permit **and** proof of filing |
| Maintained status, WP-EXT in hand | Workable | Manual underwriting; lead with the letter |
| Renewal decision pending past letter validity | Weakest | Expect a decline or a very small amount |

The fourth row is the one worth planning around. Applicants seeking loans for work permit holders Canada lenders provide often wait until the renewal is already filed and the permit already lapsed, because that is when the stress arrives. Two months earlier, the same application is routine.

A second timing point: **file your renewal as early as IRCC permits.** It is the single act that determines whether maintained status protects you at all — a filing one day late breaks it entirely — and an early filing also starts the interim letter's validity clock sooner, which means more of your loan term sits inside a period you can evidence.

If your renewal is already in progress, do not treat that as disqualifying. It simply means the four-document pack below stops being optional.

## Loans for Work Permit Holders Canada: The Four-Document Pack

Submit these together rather than waiting to be asked. Volunteering them changes how the file is read.

| Document | What it proves | What it does *not* prove |
|---|---|---|
| Work permit (even expired) | Status, employer, permit type | That you are currently authorised, once past the date |
| WP-EXT interim proof of work letter | Continued authorisation, with a **future** date | The final renewal outcome |
| Application confirmation / AOR from IRCC | You filed **before** expiry — the key fact | Anything about processing time |
| Employer letter (role, tenure, salary, ongoing) | Income continuity from the payer's side | Immigration status |

Add recent pay stubs and 60–90 days of bank statements and you have answered every question underwriting is going to ask. Applicants who assemble this pack for loans for work permit holders Canada lenders offer typically convert a "declined" into a "approved at a smaller amount" or a straight yes — the file finally contains an answer instead of a gap.

## Loans for Work Permit Holders Canada: Matching Term to Timeline

Size the loan against the period you can **evidence**, not the date printed on a document that may already be superseded.

| Your situation | Evidenced horizon | Sensible structure |
|---|---|---|
| Permit valid 18+ months | Long | Standard instalment term; fewest questions |
| Permit valid 6–12 months | Medium | Term inside the permit window, or expect a smaller amount |
| Maintained status + WP-EXT (365 days) | Up to a year from filing | Short term inside the letter's validity |
| Maintained status + WP-EXT (180-day PGWP) | Six months | Small amount, short term, or wait for the decision |
| Renewal filed late / status broken | None | Do not borrow on an assumption; resolve status first |

## Loans for Work Permit Holders Canada: Recovering From a Date Decline

A decline on this ground is one of the more recoverable ones, because it is usually an information problem.

![An IRCC interim proof of work letter beside a loan application, the document that unlocks loans for work permit holders Canada approvals](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8867433/pexels-photo-8867433.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

1. **Ask what the decline was based on.** "Status" and "expiry date" are different answers and lead to different fixes.
2. **Re-apply with the WP-EXT letter attached**, not merely mentioned — to a lender that underwrites manually.
3. **Shorten the term** so the final payment lands inside the letter's validity. This alone reverses a surprising number of declines.
4. **Reduce the amount.** The continuity risk a lender is pricing shrinks with the balance.
5. **Do not shotgun applications.** Multiple inquiries on a thin file compound the original problem — see our guide to [building a file with no credit history](/blog/loans-for-people-with-no-credit-canada) if yours is new.
