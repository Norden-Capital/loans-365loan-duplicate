**What happens if a payday loan goes to collections Canada**-wide is more structured than most borrowers expect. Collections is not a free-for-all where whoever calls loudest wins. It is a regulated sequence, and at each stage a specific right switches on in your favour. Most people never use those rights because nobody tells them the rights exist. This guide maps the timeline against the rules.

![A person taking a collection call at a desk, the reality of what happens if a payday loan goes to collections Canada borrowers face](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4467783/pexels-photo-4467783.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Quick Answer

Your account is handed to a collection agency, usually within 30 to 90 days of default. Before that agency may call you even once, it must send a written notice and then wait six calendar days. After first contact it may reach you no more than three times in seven days, never before 7 a.m. or after 9 p.m., only between 1 p.m. and 5 p.m. on Sundays, and never on a public holiday. If you dispute the debt and ask for the matter to go to court, it must stop contacting you entirely.

## What Happens if a Payday Loan Goes to Collections Canada: The Handoff

The first thing that happens if a payday loan goes to collections Canada lenders issue is a change of ownership — and there are two versions of it, which matter differently:

- **Assignment.** The agency collects on the lender's behalf. The lender remains the creditor and the balance is still governed by the original agreement.
- **Sale.** The debt is sold, often for a fraction of its face value. The buyer becomes the creditor and will usually be the name that appears on your credit file.

Either way the account can also be reported to Equifax or TransUnion — typically by the agency rather than by the payday lender, which is why the entry so often carries an unfamiliar name.

## Stage Two: The Notice You Should Have Received

Before any collection call in Ontario, the agency must send a written notice by regular mail or email containing:

- the name of the creditor
- details of the debt, including the balance and the type of product
- the name of the collection agency and of the collector
- confirmation of the agency's authority to collect
- the government's disclosure statement, *"Debt Collection: Information About Your Rights"*

It must then **wait six calendar days** before contacting you or demanding payment. If your first knowledge of the debt was a phone call, that sequence was not followed — worth noting in writing.

## Stage Three: The Contact Rules

| Rule | The limit |
|---|---|
| Contact frequency | No more than **3 times in 7 days** after first contact |
| Weekday and Saturday hours | Not between **9 p.m. and 7 a.m.** local time |
| Sundays | Only between **1 p.m. and 5 p.m.** |
| Public holidays | **No contact at all** |
| Your employer | One-time verification of employment, title and business address only — or where a garnishment or court order is involved |
| Family, household, acquaintances | Only to obtain your address or phone number |
| Charges to you | Cannot incur long-distance or text charges on your account |

The payday lender itself, if it is still collecting, is bound by a stricter provincial rule: no more than **three contacts a week**, none on holidays, and no contact with your spouse, family, household members, relatives, friends, neighbours or acquaintances at all.

## What Happens if a Payday Loan Goes to Collections Canada? Two Rights

**Right one: dispute and demand court.** If you dispute the debt and notify the agency that you want the creditor to take the matter to court, the agency must stop communicating with you. The same applies if you tell them to deal only with your lawyer or licensed paralegal. This is not a trick or a loophole — it is the published rule, and it converts an open-ended phone campaign into a decision the creditor has to make in front of a judge.

Use it honestly. If you genuinely owe the money and simply cannot pay, saying so and asking for a payment arrangement is usually the better route. But if the amount is wrong, the debt is not yours, or the fees exceed what the law allows, this right exists for exactly that situation.

**Right two: ask for the recording.** An agency with ten or more collectors must record debt-related calls, advise callers that recording is occurring, retain recordings for one year, and provide a copy **within ten days of a request, at no charge**. If a collector has threatened something they cannot do — jail, contacting your employer, seizing benefits — that recording is the evidence, and it is free.

## What to Say on the First Call

The first conversation sets the terms of everything after it, and most people improvise it badly under pressure. Five rules cover it:

1. **Do not confirm the debt is yours until you have seen the written notice.** "Please send me the notice and I will review it" is a complete answer. Acknowledging a debt in the wrong circumstances can restart a limitation period.
2. **Do not give new banking or card details.** No legitimate first call requires them, and a payment taken on the spot is a payment made before you verified the amount.
3. **Ask for the itemisation in writing** — the advance, the cost of borrowing, and each charge added since.
4. **Do not make a token payment to buy goodwill.** On an older debt this can hand the creditor a fresh two-year window to sue.
5. **Write down the collector's name, the agency, the file number, the date and the time.** If you later need the call recording, that is what you will be asked for.

Understanding what happens if a payday loan goes to collections Canada agencies handle also means understanding that the person calling is working from a script with targets attached. Being calm and slow is not rude — it is simply refusing to make decisions at someone else's pace.

## What the Collector Cannot Do

Ontario's prohibited-practices rules bar an agency from publishing or threatening to publish your failure to pay, using threatening, profane, intimidating or coercive language, giving false or misleading information, communicating with a frequency that constitutes harassment, and threatening legal action without the creditor's written authority. It must also notify you before recommending that the creditor commence proceedings.

Two claims that come up often and are wrong: a collector cannot have you arrested for the debt, and a payday lender cannot take money from your pay without first suing, obtaining a judgment and then a garnishment order.

## Check the Numbers Before You Pay

Part of what happens if a payday loan goes to collections Canada borrowers should expect is arithmetic drift: balances are not automatically correct. Test them against the caps:

- **Cost of borrowing:** capped federally at **14% of the amount advanced** since 1 January 2025 — $14 per $100 — in every designated province.
- **Default interest:** maximum **2.5% per month**, non-compounding.
- **Dishonoured-payment fee:** maximum **$20**.

Ask for a written breakdown showing the advance, the cost of borrowing, and every charge added since. An amount that cannot be itemised is an amount to question. And if the lender was not licensed in Ontario, the borrower is only required to repay the advance — the cost of borrowing is not owed at all.

## Your Next Five Moves

1. **Put everything in writing** and keep copies. Phone calls evaporate; letters do not.
2. **Request the itemised breakdown** before discussing any payment.
3. **Pull both credit reports** and check the first delinquency date, which sets the six-year expiry.
4. **Ask about the limitation period** — in most provinces a creditor has two years from your last payment or acknowledgment to sue, and a small payment can restart it.
5. **Complain if the rules were broken**, to your provincial consumer protection office. Agencies are licensed, and licences matter to them.

## The Bottom Line

Knowing **what happens if a payday loan goes to collections Canada** borrowers can expect turns an intimidating process into a checklist. The agency must write first and wait six days. It gets three contacts a week, within set hours. It cannot involve your family or, in most cases, your employer. You can require it to stop and go to court, and you can obtain the recording of any call for free. Verify the arithmetic before you pay anything.
