A **line of credit for bad credit Canada** lenders will actually approve exists, it is legal, and its price is already decided for you: **35% APR is the hard ceiling**, and most subprime revolving products sit at 34.99% because that is the legal maximum. The real question is not who will approve you — several lenders will. It is whether revolving credit is the right *shape* for your problem, because a line of credit is the one borrowing product that can quietly lower your credit score while you make every payment on time.

## What a line of credit for bad credit Canada lenders offer actually is

A line of credit is a **revolving** limit. You are approved for a maximum — often $500 to $5,000 in the subprime market — and you draw what you need, pay interest only on the drawn balance, repay, and draw again. There is no fixed end date and no fixed payment. Minimum payments are usually interest plus a small percentage of the balance.

That is a completely different animal from an installment loan, which hands you a lump sum with a fixed payment and a fixed payoff date. Both are advertised to the same audience, and the difference matters more than the rate.

Here is the part most comparison articles skip. When you have damaged credit, the flexibility that makes a line of credit attractive is the same feature that makes it dangerous. An installment loan forces you to finish. A line of credit lets you carry a balance forever, and at 34.99% a permanent balance is a permanent tax on your income.

## The 35% ceiling that sets the price of every line of credit for bad credit Canada offers

Since **January 1, 2025**, the criminal rate of interest under section 347 of the Criminal Code has been **35% APR**, down from the old 60% effective annual rate of roughly 48% APR. Licensed payday loans are carved out of that ceiling and governed separately by a $14-per-$100 cost cap. **Lines of credit are not carved out.**

That single fact explains the entire subprime pricing landscape:

- If a lender advertises 34.99% on a revolving limit, they are charging the legal maximum.
- If a lender advertises above 35% APR on a line of credit, something is wrong — either the product is not what it claims to be, or mandatory fees are being excluded from the stated rate.
- The "total cost of borrowing" must include mandatory membership, administration and insurance fees. A 29.9% rate plus a compulsory $10 monthly fee on a $500 balance is not 29.9%.

For context on the other end of the market, the Bank of Canada held its policy rate at **2.25%** on July 15, 2026 — a sixth consecutive hold — which keeps prime at **4.45%**. A prime-linked personal line of credit for a borrower with strong credit therefore runs in the single digits. The gap between that and 34.99% is the actual cost of bad credit, and it is roughly 30 percentage points.

## 6 smart, safe moves before you open one

### 1. Decide if you need a limit or a lump sum

Ask one question: **is this expense finished?** A furnace repair, a dental bill or a moving cost is finished — it has a known number, and an installment loan with a fixed payoff date fits it better. An irregular income gap across a slow season is not finished, and that is where revolving credit genuinely earns its keep.

Choosing a line of credit for a one-time expense is the most common and most expensive mistake in this category — and it is the reason many people who open a line of credit for bad credit Canada lenders approve end up paying interest years after the original bill was settled.

### 2. Price the fees, not the rate

Build the real annual cost before you compare anything:

- Annual or monthly maintenance fee
- Draw or transaction fees per advance
- Optional-but-defaulted balance protection insurance
- Inactivity fees

On a $1,000 limit with a $600 average balance, a $9 monthly fee adds 18% to your effective annual cost on top of the stated interest. Two offers on a line of credit for bad credit Canada lenders quote at the same 34.99% can differ by hundreds of dollars a year on fees alone.

### 3. Check that the lender reports to a bureau

This cuts both ways, and it is why it belongs on the list. A lender that reports to Equifax or TransUnion gives you a path out of subprime pricing: 12 months of clean revolving history is one of the fastest score repairs available. A lender that does not report gives you the cost of bad credit with none of the repair.

Ask directly: *"Do you report to Equifax, TransUnion, or both?"* Get the answer in writing. Reporting is the one feature that separates a line of credit for bad credit Canada borrowers can grow out of from one they simply pay for.

### 4. Set your own limit below the approved limit

If you are approved for $3,000, decide up front that your working ceiling is $900 and treat the rest as unavailable. Utilization is calculated on your reported balance against the limit, so a high approved limit is only helpful if you leave it unused.

### 5. Automate more than the minimum

Minimum payments on revolving credit are engineered to keep the balance alive. On a $1,500 balance at 34.99% with a 3% minimum, you are paying roughly $44 in interest in month one against a $45 payment. Set a fixed automatic payment — $150, $200, whatever is real — instead of accepting the minimum.

### 6. Book a re-price date

Put a date in your calendar six months out. If your score has moved, ask for a limit increase at a lower rate or refinance the balance into a fixed installment loan. Subprime lenders almost never volunteer a lower rate. You have to ask, and most borrowers never do.

## Line of credit vs the alternatives: what actually costs less

| Product | Typical cost, bad credit | Payoff date | Reports to bureau | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Line of credit (subprime) | Up to 34.99% APR + fees | None — revolving | Usually | Irregular, repeating gaps |
| Installment loan | 19.99%–34.99% APR | Fixed | Usually | One finished expense |
| Bank overdraft protection | ~$5/month or per use + 21%–22% | None | No | Very short, small shortfalls |
| Licensed payday loan | $14 per $100 (max 62 days) | Next payday | Rarely | Last resort only |
| Credit-builder loan | Low rate, funds held in trust | Fixed | Always | Repair, not cash access |

Two things stand out. First, a subprime **line of credit for bad credit Canada** lenders price at the ceiling is *not* dramatically cheaper than a subprime installment loan — the ceiling binds both. Second, the overdraft column changed this year, which matters more than it looks.

## The rule change that quietly reshaped this decision in 2026

On **March 12, 2026**, new federal regulations capped non-sufficient funds (NSF) fees at **$10**, down from as much as $50. Banks also cannot charge an NSF fee more than once in two business days on the same account, and cannot charge one at all when the shortfall is under $10.

Why this belongs in an article about a line of credit for bad credit Canada borrowers are considering: a large share of people open subprime revolving credit purely as **NSF insurance** — a buffer so a rent cheque or pre-authorized debit never bounces. That insurance used to be worth a lot when a single bounce cost $48. At $10 a bounce, opening a 34.99% revolving limit to avoid two NSF charges a year is now clearly the more expensive choice.

If NSF protection is your only reason for wanting a limit, the arithmetic no longer supports it. If cash-flow smoothing across genuinely irregular income is your reason, it still does.

## The utilization trap nobody warns you about

This is the trap in the headline, and it catches careful borrowers, not careless ones.

Revolving credit is reported to the bureaus with two numbers: your balance and your limit. The ratio between them is your **utilization**, and it is one of the heaviest factors in a credit score. Installment loans are not scored this way — a $1,000 personal loan does not create a utilization ratio.

So consider two borrowers who each need $900 and each pay perfectly for a year:

- **Borrower A** takes a $1,000 installment loan. Their score generally improves from month one on payment history.
- **Borrower B** draws $900 on a $1,000 line of credit. They are reported at **90% utilization**. Their payment history improves — and their utilization score component gets worse, often enough to cancel the gain out entirely.

Borrower B did nothing wrong. They chose the product with the flexible terms and were punished by how that product is measured — which is the single most under-explained risk in any line of credit for bad credit Canada comparison you will read. The fix is mechanical: keep the reported balance under **30%** of the limit, and if you cannot, take the installment loan instead. ## Who should skip a line of credit entirely

Be honest about which of these describes you:

- **You are covering a shortfall that repeats every month.** Revolving credit will not fix a structural gap between income and expenses; it will finance it at 35% until it collapses. A [debt consolidation loan](/loans/by-type/debt-consolidation-loans) or a credit counsellor is the correct call.
- **You already carry a balance on another revolving account.** Adding a second utilization ratio compounds the score problem.
- **Your income is stable and your expense is one-time.** Take the fixed loan. You will pay less and finish.
- **You are within 12 months of applying for a mortgage.** Open revolving credit reduces borrowing power even at a zero balance, because lenders stress-test the full limit.

If none of those apply and you genuinely have lumpy income, a modest line of credit for bad credit Canada lenders offer, used deliberately, is a reasonable tool. ## 5 questions to ask before you sign

Print these and work through them with the lender on the phone. Every one of them has changed an outcome for a real borrower.

1. **"What is the total cost of borrowing as an APR, including every mandatory fee?"** Any line of credit for bad credit Canada lenders offer must land at or under 35% APR once fees are included. If they will only quote a rate and not an all-in number, that is your answer.
2. **"Do you report to Equifax, TransUnion, or both?"** Without reporting, you pay subprime pricing and your file never improves.
3. **"Is there an inactivity fee or a minimum draw?"** Some subprime revolving products charge you for *not* using the limit, which quietly destroys the "open it as a backup" strategy.
4. **"Can I request a limit increase or a rate review, and after how long?"** Get the timeline in writing and diarise it. Lenders do not volunteer lower rates.
5. **"What happens if I miss a payment — what is the fee and when is it reported?"** A 30-day late mark stays on your file for roughly six years. Knowing the lender's grace window before you need it is worth more than any promotional rate.

A lender who answers all five plainly is one worth dealing with. A lender who deflects on the first one is telling you what the total cost of borrowing actually looks like.

## Key takeaways

- The price of a **line of credit for bad credit Canada** lenders approve is capped at **35% APR**, so 34.99% is the ceiling, not a bargain.
- Revolving credit suits **repeating, irregular** shortfalls. One-time expenses belong on a fixed installment loan with a payoff date.
- Mandatory fees must be counted in the total cost — a 29.9% rate with a compulsory monthly fee is not 29.9%.
- The **$10 NSF cap** that took effect March 12, 2026 removed the main defensive reason to open one.
- Watch **utilization**, not just payments. Under 30% of the limit is the threshold that protects your score.
- Ask whether the lender reports to Equifax or TransUnion. Without reporting, you get the cost of bad credit and none of the repair.
